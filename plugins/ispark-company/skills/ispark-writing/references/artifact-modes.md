# Artifact Modes

Apply the shared fact ledger, then adapt to the artifact's real job.

## Durable documentation

Optimize for retrieval and maintenance. Put the current fact, boundary, command, or decision near the heading a future reader will search. Keep historical narrative out of current-fact indexes unless the history explains a live constraint.

Use `$ispark-docs-issues` for document placement, source-of-truth rules, and required structure.

## PRD

Name the user, problem, current workaround, desired behavior, non-goals, edge cases, and success evidence. Do not replace an unknown user need with a feature slogan or invent metrics to fill a success section.

## Technical decision or design note

Lead with context, decision, alternatives, consequences, and open questions. Preserve exact identifiers, contract terms, failure modes, and compatibility language. Explain mechanisms before claiming benefits.

## Decision memo or founder note

Lead with the decision or decision required. Separate evidence, judgment, and unknowns. Keep private strategic context within its authorized boundary; do not turn personal judgment into a default company fact.

## Release note or announcement

State the user-visible change, evidence, limitations, and release posture. Distinguish built, validated, installable, deployed, published, and generally available. Use `$ispark-release-ops` for the actual branch, artifact, rollout, and smoke facts.

## Incident or operator communication

Prioritize time, impact, affected scope, evidence, mitigation, current state, and next checkpoint. Avoid blame, speculative causality, and polished reassurance that is not supported by recovery evidence.

## Collaboration message

Give the recipient the minimum context needed, then the request, deadline or checkpoint, and expected response. Do not expand a direct question into a report. Use `$ispark-lark` for recipients, permissions, resource operations, and sending.

## Hiring or evaluation artifact

Keep observation, evidence, inference, and decision separate. Do not use eloquence to strengthen a weak signal or smooth conflicting interviewer evidence. Preserve privacy and the role's actual rubric.

## Product and UI copy

Treat words as part of the interaction:

- Buttons name the action: `Save changes` is more specific than `Submit`.
- Labels identify the requested value, not its general category.
- Links make sense without surrounding prose.
- Errors state what happened, why when known, and what the user can do next.
- Empty states name what is absent and offer the single relevant next action.
- Loading text reflects the observed duration and real operation.
- Product claims use supplied features and evidence; never fabricate proof, testimonials, or metrics.

Use `$ispark-product-design` for workflow, hierarchy, visual system, and interaction decisions. Verify rendered copy fit through `$ispark-browser-qa` when the text appears in a frontend.

## Public or brand writing

Respect existing brand language before introducing a new voice. Prefer specific nouns, verbs, places, dates, product forms, and real constraints over generic aspiration. A memorable phrase cannot substitute for a true claim.

## Academic or research prose

This skill may improve clarity and structure, but it is not an academic authority. The canonical manuscript, evidence protocol, citation rules, and claim denominators remain with the target project. Never add citations, results, novelty claims, or causal language from general knowledge alone.
