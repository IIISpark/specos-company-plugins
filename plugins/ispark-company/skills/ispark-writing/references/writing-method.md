# Writing Method

## Establish the writing contract

Before drafting or editing, identify only the fields that change the result:

- reader and what they already know
- purpose and the action or decision the prose should support
- channel, length, language, and publication state
- source facts and literals that must remain exact
- allowed inference, open questions, and prohibited claims
- existing brand, author, legal, academic, or repository voice that outranks this skill

Do not turn this into a questionnaire. Inspect supplied material first. Ask one focused question only when a missing field would change facts, authority, external exposure, or the requested action.

## Lock the fact ledger

Classify source material before changing prose:

- `Verified`: explicitly supplied or supported by an authoritative source.
- `Inferred`: a reasonable interpretation that must remain marked as inference.
- `Unknown`: missing, disputed, or not yet verified.
- `Literal`: names, commands, identifiers, dates, metrics, quotations, citations, contractual language, or status terms that must remain exact.

Style work must not move a claim from `Inferred` to `Verified`, replace `Unknown` with a plausible detail, or turn `may` into `will`. When sources conflict, report the conflict instead of blending them into a smooth sentence.

## Draft

1. Write the controlling point in one plain sentence.
2. Order information by the reader's task, not by the order in which research was gathered.
3. Give each paragraph one job: establish context, make a claim, provide evidence, name a consequence, or request an action.
4. Use concrete nouns and verbs. Name the system, person, artifact, event, decision, and boundary when known.
5. Include only evidence that changes understanding or confidence.
6. End when the reader has enough information to act. Do not add a generic conclusion to make the artifact feel complete.

## Rewrite

1. Record the fact ledger and the existing author's actual position.
2. Fix argument order before polishing sentences.
3. Remove repetition, stock framing, empty transitions, and unsupported intensifiers.
4. Replace abstract noun chains with actors and actions where the source supports them.
5. Preserve useful terminology and intentional voice. Do not normalize every author into the same company tone.
6. Compare the result against the source for names, numbers, claim strength, omissions, and newly introduced implications.

If a stronger rewrite would require a new fact or a substantive position change, surface that as an editorial question rather than silently making it.

## Adapt

Adaptation may change ordering, density, vocabulary, and formality. It must not change evidence or publication status.

- Shortening: remove context the target reader already has before removing caveats or evidence.
- Externalizing: remove private detail and internal shorthand; do not convert an internal plan into a shipped claim.
- Making technical prose accessible: explain terms and consequences; do not replace precise mechanisms with marketing language.
- Making prose executive-readable: lead with the decision, consequence, and evidence boundary; retain links or pointers to detail.
- Changing tone: vary cadence and diction, not the underlying degree of confidence.

## Final pass

Check:

- Every factual statement is supported, attributed, or marked as inference.
- The main point appears before secondary context.
- Each heading and paragraph earns its place.
- Lists are used for parallel items, not to avoid writing a coherent argument.
- The result has no duplicated summary, invented metric, placeholder testimonial, or false certainty.
- The prose fits the named reader and channel rather than a generic professional voice.
- Any unresolved question is explicit and proportionate to its impact.

For audit-only requests, do not run the rewrite steps. Use `anti-slop.md` and return findings only.
